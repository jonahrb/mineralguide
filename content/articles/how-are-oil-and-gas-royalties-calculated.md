Oil and gas royalties are calculated by multiplying the production attributable to your mineral interest by the price received for that production and your royalty ownership percentage.

**The basic formula is:**

**Royalty Payment = Production × Price per Unit × Royalty Decimal**

For example, if your share of a well's production is 100 barrels of oil, the oil sells for $70 per barrel, and your royalty interest is 25%, your gross royalty is:

100 barrels × $70 × 25% = **$1,750**

That is the basic calculation before applicable taxes and any permitted deductions.

The most important number to understand is your **royalty decimal**. It represents the percentage of the well's revenue that belongs to you.

## 1. How Is Your Royalty Percentage Determined?

Your royalty percentage is determined by two things:

1. How much of the mineral rights you own in the producing area.
2. The royalty rate negotiated in the oil and gas lease.

The formula is:

**Royalty Decimal = (Your Net Mineral Acres ÷ Total Unit Acres) × Lease Royalty Rate**

For example, suppose you own 10 net mineral acres in a 640-acre drilling unit, and the lease provides a 25% royalty.

| Calculation | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Your net mineral acres | 10 |
| Total unit acres | 640 |
| Your ownership fraction | 10 ÷ 640 = 1.5625% |
| Lease royalty rate | 25% |
| **Your royalty decimal** | **0.00390625** |

Your royalty decimal is 0.00390625, meaning you receive 0.390625% of the applicable well revenue.

Notice that owning 10 acres does not mean you receive 10% of the royalties. Your ownership is divided by the total acreage in the unit, then multiplied by the lease royalty rate.

### What Is a Net Mineral Acre?

A net mineral acre (NMA) represents one acre of full mineral ownership.

If you own 50% of the minerals under 20 acres, you own 10 net mineral acres.

| Gross Acres | Ownership | Net Mineral Acres |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20 | 100% | 20 |
| 20 | 50% | 10 |
| 40 | 25% | 10 |
| 100 | 10% | 10 |

Net mineral acres are important because they determine your proportional ownership in a drilling unit.

## 2. How Are Oil Royalties Calculated?

Oil royalties are calculated using the number of barrels attributable to your interest and the price received per barrel.

The formula is:

**Oil Royalty = Barrels Produced × Price per Barrel × Royalty Decimal**

For example, assume:

- The well produces 1,000 barrels of oil during the month.
- The oil sells for $70 per barrel.
- Your royalty decimal is 0.00390625.

Your payment is:

1,000 × $70 × 0.00390625 = **$273.44**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Monthly well production | 1,000 barrels |
| Oil price | $70/barrel |
| Gross well revenue | $70,000 |
| Your royalty decimal | 0.00390625 |
| **Your gross royalty** | **$273.44** |

Your actual payment may be lower after applicable production taxes and permitted deductions.

## 3. How Are Natural Gas Royalties Calculated?

Natural gas royalties use the same basic formula, but production is measured in gas volume rather than barrels of oil.

Gas production is commonly reported in Mcf, meaning one thousand cubic feet of natural gas.

**Gas Royalty = Mcf Produced × Price per Mcf × Royalty Decimal**

For example, assume:

- The well produces 10,000 Mcf of gas during the month.
- The gas sells for $3 per Mcf.
- Your royalty decimal is 0.00390625.

Your payment is:

10,000 × $3 × 0.00390625 = **$117.19**

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Monthly well production | 10,000 Mcf |
| Gas price | $3/Mcf |
| Gross well revenue | $30,000 |
| Your royalty decimal | 0.00390625 |
| **Your gross royalty** | **$117.19** |

Gas prices are typically quoted in dollars per MMBtu in commodity markets, while royalty statements may report gas volumes and prices using different units. The operator's reported sales price and volume must use compatible units for the calculation.

## 4. How Much Do You Make Per Net Mineral Acre?

There is no single royalty amount per net mineral acre because the payment depends on production, commodity prices, lease royalty rate, and the size of the drilling unit.

However, you can calculate the royalty value per net mineral acre using the same formula.

For example, assume a 640-acre unit produces 1,000 barrels of oil per month at $70 per barrel, with a 25% lease royalty.

Total monthly gross well revenue:

1,000 × $70 = $70,000

Total monthly royalty pool:

$70,000 × 25% = $17,500

Monthly royalty per net mineral acre:

$17,500 ÷ 640 = **$27.34 per net mineral acre**

An owner with 10 net mineral acres would receive approximately $273.44 per month before applicable deductions.

This is a useful way to compare royalty income across different mineral interests, provided you account for differences in production, pricing, and lease terms.

## 5. What Deductions Are Taken Out of Oil and Gas Royalties?

The gross royalty calculation is not always the amount deposited into your bank account.

Depending on the lease and applicable law, royalty payments may be reduced by certain taxes and permitted expenses.

Common items include:

- **Production taxes:** State taxes imposed on oil and gas production.
- **Gathering:** Costs to transport production from the well to a processing or sales facility.
- **Compression:** Costs associated with compressing natural gas for transportation.
- **Processing:** Costs to process natural gas and remove marketable components or impurities.
- **Transportation:** Costs to move production to a sales point.

Whether an operator can deduct these expenses depends on the lease language and applicable law. Some leases contain no-deductions or free-of-cost provisions, while others expressly permit certain deductions.

Do not assume every deduction on a royalty statement is automatically permitted. Review the lease and the applicable deduction provisions.

## 6. Why Do Two Mineral Owners in the Same Well Receive Different Royalty Payments?

Two mineral owners in the same well can receive very different payments because their royalty decimals may be different.

The most common reasons are:

**Different ownership amounts.** Someone who owns 20 net mineral acres receives twice the royalty of someone who owns 10 net mineral acres, assuming the same unit and lease terms.

**Different lease royalty rates.** One owner may have negotiated a 25% royalty while another has a 20% royalty.

**Different acreage or unit participation.** The amount of production allocated to an owner's tract can affect the royalty calculation, especially in horizontal wells.

**Different deductions or lease provisions.** Owners may have different lease language governing post-production costs.

**Different ownership burdens.** Existing royalty interests, overriding royalty interests, and other ownership arrangements can affect the amount attributable to a particular interest.

The royalty decimal on your division order is one of the best places to start when checking whether your payment is correct.

## 7. How Do You Calculate Your Own Monthly Oil and Gas Royalties?

You only need three primary numbers:

1. Your royalty decimal.
2. Your share of monthly production, or the total production allocated to the relevant well or unit.
3. The price received per unit of production.

Then multiply them together.

For example:

| Input | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Monthly production | 5,000 barrels |
| Price per barrel | $65 |
| Royalty decimal | 0.0025 |
| Gross monthly royalty | **$812.50** |

Calculation:

5,000 × $65 × 0.0025 = $812.50

Your actual payment will reflect applicable taxes and permitted deductions.

If you already know your royalty decimal, you do not need to calculate your net mineral acres or lease royalty rate again. You can use the decimal directly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What percentage of oil and gas production goes to mineral owners?

A common lease royalty rate is 12.5%, 18.75%, 20%, or 25%. The actual rate is established by the lease. Your individual share is smaller because it is also affected by how much of the drilling unit's minerals you own.

### What is a royalty decimal?

A royalty decimal is the fraction of a well's applicable revenue that belongs to you. For example, a decimal of 0.005 means you receive 0.5% of the applicable revenue.

### How do I calculate my royalty decimal?

Divide your net mineral acres by the total unit acres, then multiply by your lease royalty rate.

For example, 10 net mineral acres in a 640-acre unit with a 25% royalty:

(10 ÷ 640) × 0.25 = 0.00390625.

### Are oil and gas royalties paid monthly?

Most operators pay royalties monthly, although payment timing and minimum payment thresholds can vary. Payments are generally made after production is reported and revenue is calculated.

### Do mineral owners receive royalties on gross or net production?

Royalty calculations generally begin with the production and revenue attributable to the mineral owner's interest. The amount ultimately paid may be reduced by applicable production taxes and deductions permitted under the lease and applicable law.

### Do higher oil and gas prices increase royalty payments?

Yes. Assuming production and your royalty decimal remain unchanged, higher sales prices increase your royalty payments proportionally.

For example, if your royalty payment is $1,000 at $50 per barrel, a price increase to $60 per barrel would increase the payment to approximately $1,200, assuming the same production and no change in other adjustments.

### Can I check whether my royalty payments are correct?

Yes. Start with your division order to find your royalty decimal. Then compare the production volume, sales price, and deductions on your royalty statement against the applicable lease terms.

## Understand the Value of Your Mineral Rights

Your monthly royalty income is one of the most important factors in determining the value of your mineral interests.

If you receive $1,000 per month in royalties, that represents $12,000 in annual income. The value of the underlying mineral rights can be substantially greater than a single year's royalty payments because the minerals may continue producing for years and may have additional development potential.

If you own producing mineral rights, understanding your royalty income is also the first step in evaluating your financing options.

**You may be able to borrow against your mineral interests while retaining ownership and continuing to receive your royalty payments.** This can provide access to cash without permanently selling an income-producing asset.
