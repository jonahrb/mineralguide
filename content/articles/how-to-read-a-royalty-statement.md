A royalty statement is a payment record for a stated property and sales period. It can help you check how the payor calculated a payment, but the exact line items and timing depend on the state, the lease or other agreement, the interest type, and the payor's reporting format.

## Start with the property and period

Check the well, lease, property number, county, and state shown on the statement. Then confirm the sales month or other period covered. Payors may use an internal property number that differs from a regulator's well identifier, so keep the legal description and any operator identifiers with your records.

## Review the production and value lines

Many statements include some combination of volume sold, price per unit, taxes, other deductions or adjustments, net value, owner decimal, and gross or net proceeds. Texas law specifies certain statement components for covered payments; the Texas Railroad Commission's royalties FAQ lists examples such as property identification, sales month and year, volume, price, taxes, other deductions or adjustments, and the owner's decimal and proceeds.

Compare the statement's units and sales period before comparing it with a public production report. Regulator data is reported by the operator and may use different identifiers or timing from the payment statement. Differences do not automatically show an error; they identify a question to resolve with the payor.

## Check the owner decimal and interest type

The owner decimal represents the payor's stated share for that property and interest. Compare it with the most recent division order or title information you have, and check that the interest type (for example, royalty or overriding royalty) is labeled as expected. A change in decimal may follow a transfer, title update, pooling or unit change, correction, or a change in the property allocation; ask the payor to explain the change.

## Understand deductions and adjustments

Look for taxes, transportation, processing, compression, prior-period corrections, or other items. Whether a particular charge is allowed depends on the lease or other governing document and the applicable law. If an entry is unfamiliar, ask the payor to identify the charge, the period it covers, and the document or calculation it relies on.

## Keep a simple review record

For each statement, save the original document and note the property, sales period, owner decimal, payment date, and any questions you sent to the payor. If you compare production data, record the regulator identifier and the period you queried. This makes later adjustments easier to follow.

For a Texas payment, the Railroad Commission's FAQ describes statutory statement requirements and explains ways owners may request information. Requirements differ elsewhere, so check the state and the specific documents that govern your interest.

This guide is general education and does not determine whether a deduction or payment is correct for a specific interest.
