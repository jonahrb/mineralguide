This calculator helps you compare two different outcomes: borrowing while keeping the modeled royalty income, or receiving sale proceeds and giving up the portion of future royalties included in the offer. A loan may fit a temporary cash need if its full repayment terms work for you. A sale may fit if you prefer not to take on debt and are comfortable transferring the rights described in the agreement.

## How the comparison works

The tool estimates the present value of scheduled loan payments, then compares the loan's net cash and payments with the sale offer and the future royalty income assumed to transfer in that sale. A positive comparison means borrowing has the higher modeled value under the inputs; a negative comparison means selling has the higher modeled value. The recommendation is a scenario result, not a personal financial recommendation.

The loan uses a standard fully amortizing monthly payment. With no interest, the financed balance is divided by the number of payments. Otherwise, the payment is calculated from the monthly interest rate and term. The tool discounts each payment using the annual discount rate converted to an equivalent monthly rate.

## How loan fees affect the result

Loan fees default to 3% of the requested loan amount. For example, on a $50,000 request, the estimated fee is $1,500. If paid upfront, the estimated cash received is $48,500 while payments are calculated on $50,000. If rolled into the loan, the estimated cash received remains $50,000, but payments are calculated on a $51,500 balance. Change the fee rate under Advanced assumptions to match an offer you are reviewing.

## Royalty income assumptions

The tool estimates future income from the current monthly royalty amount, the percentage assumed transferred in a sale, the annual decline assumption, the projection period, and the annual discount rate. It applies each year's decline to that year's monthly payments. It does not forecast new wells, commodity-price changes, production interruptions, or property-specific lease deductions.

## What this calculator leaves out

The result does not include taxes, title issues, collateral and default remedies, transaction costs beyond the estimated loan fee, changing loan terms, balloon payments, a future resale value for retained minerals, or changes in production and commodity prices. It assumes a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments and no balloon payment.

For a broader discussion of the tradeoffs, read [Should I sell my mineral rights or get a loan?](/learn/sell-mineral-rights-or-get-a-loan).

## Does a higher modeled value mean I should choose that option?

No. The result only compares the assumptions entered. Cash-flow needs, ability to make payments, contract terms, taxes, ownership documents, and risk tolerance can change which option fits. Consider having the written offers reviewed by qualified independent professionals.
